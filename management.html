Science management & policy

Alongside my research, I hold a Master of Public Administration (MPA) in science management and am engaged in questions of research funding, evaluation, and policy. I am available for reviewing assignments, advisory boards, and panels.

My engagement in this area has grown naturally out of my research. Leading international collaborations has taught me that scientific excellence often requires crossing boundaries — between disciplines, institutions, and countries. The initiatives I have helped establish are built on this conviction: an international Max Planck Center connecting partners in Germany, the US, and Taiwan creates a long-term institutional framework for cooperation that no national context could provide alone, while privately funded fellowship and internship programmes bring students from around the world to Munich — fostering early-career mobility, capacity building, and durable people-to-people ties between scientific communities. This is also what draws me to the concept of Science Diplomacy: international scientific cooperation both enables science of a quality no country could achieve by itself, and builds trust between societies — something I consider especially valuable in a changing geopolitical landscape. To these questions I bring the dual perspective of an active researcher and a trained science manager, together with first-hand experience of exchanges between science and policymaking at the national and European level.
